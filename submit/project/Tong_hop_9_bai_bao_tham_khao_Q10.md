## LIT-001 - Randomizing Set-Associative Caches Against Conflict-Based Cache Side-Channel Attacks

**Authors:** Wei Song, Zihan Xue, Jinchi Han, Zhenzhen Li, and Peng Liu. **Publication:** IEEE Transactions on Computers, 73(4), 1019-1033, 2024. **Assigned responsibility:** Trương Tô Dân, QE210127. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TC.2024.3349659). [Author full text](https://wsong83.github.io/publication/comparch/tc2024.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: the paper implements a randomized LLC on Rocket-Chip, with Spike/FPGA checks and SPEC CPU2006 evaluation. Sections V-VI and Figures 13 and 16-18 describe its implementation and evaluation. The measured objective is security protection with implementation overhead, not a direct-mapped sensor-cache speedup.

Song and colleagues investigate randomized placement in a conventional set-associative last-level cache as a defense against conflict-based side-channel attacks. Their design combines a hash for set selection, periodic remapping, relocation, and attack detection. The paper includes implementation in a Linux-capable multicore processor rather than treating an address expression as a complete hardware design. Its central objective is resistance to adversarial eviction-set construction. This objective differs from reducing the miss rate of a continuous sensor stream. The relevant evidence is the paper's architectural treatment of indexing and its implementation/evaluation sections, not a prediction that any randomized or XOR-based mapping improves ordinary application performance.

The distinction between placement and replacement is central to its relevance. Placement identifies the locations in which a block may reside. Replacement chooses a victim among eligible residents. Changing the index can alter which addresses compete without changing the number of data lines. This provides a coherent explanation for the project's W5 result: addresses separated by 4 KiB collide under direct mapping, while the selected XOR expression disperses the specified competing group. That explanation follows from the implemented address equations and measured counters. It does not require a claim that the project reproduces the paper's randomized security mechanism or inherits its resistance to attacks.

The project uses a static index, unlike a periodically remapped organization. A static index avoids the additional question of how previously resident blocks remain identifiable when the mapping changes. Its implementation still requires a correct inverse. For DAI-v1, the tag contains the block number shifted right by six; the set mixes the low six block bits with the next group. Reconstruction recovers the original block before dirty eviction. A wrong inverse can preserve apparent hit behavior on short traces while writing a dirty line to the wrong address. This is why the inverse-writeback checks contribute evidence beyond a favorable aggregate miss rate.

Resource comparison also requires care. DAI-v1 has two ways per set, whereas D0 has one. A direct comparison changes associativity as well as the index. Conventional two-way D1 therefore provides a necessary control for interpreting which differences follow from set regrouping. Conventional four-way D2 provides another point of comparison. All four designs retain 4,096 data bytes and 32-byte lines. Their logical metadata estimates differ. The candidate uses 376 bytes, compared with 352 bytes for D0 and 376 bytes for D1. These are explicit packed-state estimates, not measured silicon costs or process-independent implementation constants.

The measured results limit the apparent benefit. Sequential read and write streams have a 12.5% miss rate in every design. Circular buffers, moving-average traces, and the selected four-sensor arrangement also produce identical rates across the organizations for each corresponding case. DAI-v1's advantage over direct mapping appears only in W5. The result is therefore consistent with a placement-specific mechanism rather than a general improvement in sensor processing. A title or abstract describing the candidate as universally better would exceed both the project's data and the methodological lesson supplied by the related publication.

A second limitation concerns adversarial examples. Blocks 0, 65, and 130 collide in candidate set zero but occupy distinct conventional sets in the small validation counterexample. Repeating those blocks can therefore disadvantage the XOR design even though the official W5 cases favor it. This counterexample is implementation-level evidence of conditional behavior; it was not inserted into the official matrix after observing the results. Its role is to explain the boundary of the claim. The existence of favorable and unfavorable mappings is compatible with the same mathematical index rule and prevents treating the word randomized or hashed as an assurance of improvement.

Hardware feasibility remains separate from software correctness. Computing an XOR in Python establishes neither a single-cycle physical lookup nor an acceptable critical path. Two-way lookup also requires tag comparisons and selection logic. The study did not synthesize that logic or measure hardware latency, energy, or area because time and financial resources were insufficient. The AMAT calculations therefore vary assumed lookup time rather than reporting measured speed. When miss rates are equal, an additional assumed lookup cost makes the candidate's estimated access time worse. This conditional calculation supplies a meaningful trade-off without inventing an implementation cost.

The publication is retained as a direct mapping-related reference. Its contribution to the project is the treatment of indexing as an architectural choice with state, identification, and implementation consequences. Its security objective and measured hardware context remain distinct from the trace-driven sensor model. The project's scientific conclusion rests on original trace bytes, counters, and the frozen comparison, not on transferring the paper's evaluation numbers to a 4-KiB cache. The reference supports an explicit mechanism-and-cost argument while leaving the practical suitability of the candidate for a real weighing station unresolved.

### Study conclusion

**Randomizing Set-Associative Caches Against Conflict-Based Cache Side-Channel Attacks** supports the discussion of alternative indexing and implementation costs. It does not establish a security guarantee or general speedup for DAI-v1. Within BATCH01, the candidate's demonstrated benefit remains confined to the declared conflict stress patterns.

## LIT-002 - The Maya Cache: A Storage-efficient and Secure Fully-associative Last-level Cache

**Authors:** Anubhav Bhatla, Navneet, and Biswabandan Panda. **Publication:** ISCA 2024, 32-44, 2024. **Assigned responsibility:** Trương Tô Dân, QE210127. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISCA59077.2024.00013). [Author full text](https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/~biswa/ISCA2024.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: ChampSim experiments include an eight-core baseline with a 16-MiB, 16-way LLC and 64-byte lines, using SPEC CPU2017 and GAP. Sections III and V and Tables V-X describe design and comparison conditions. The separated tag/data budget differs from Q10's equal-data-capacity constraint.

Maya investigates storage-efficient secure last-level caching through a separation of tag and data resources. The design uses additional tag entries and fewer data entries, exploiting information about reuse while pursuing security goals associated with randomized placement. Its comparison demonstrates that tag capacity and data capacity need not increase together. The paper's organization, security analysis, and evaluation concern a substantially different scale and objective from the present single-level model. The relevant methodological contribution is precise resource accounting. Its results do not imply that a smaller sensor cache obtains the same storage, power, or performance behavior merely by changing an index function.

Equal capacity has several possible meanings in a cache experiment. Equal data bytes hold the amount of resident payload constant. Equal total storage additionally constrains tags, validity, dirty flags, replacement state, and any auxiliary predictor. Equal area requires an implementation model that includes circuitry beyond stored bits. BATCH01 adopts the first interpretation: all designs have 4,096 data bytes. Metadata is reported separately. This choice is appropriate for isolating behavior under a declared data-capacity constraint, but it does not establish equality of total area or hardware cost. The distinction affects the strength of every resource-related conclusion in the report.

The project's metadata estimates can be derived directly from the organization. With a 32-bit byte address and a 32-byte line, a block identifier has 27 bits. D0 uses seven set bits, leaving 20 tag bits per line, plus valid and dirty bits. Across 128 lines this gives 352 bytes. D1 and DAI-v1 use six set bits, 21 tag bits, valid and dirty bits, and one LRU bit per two-way set, giving 376 bytes. D2 uses five set bits and two rank bits per line for exact four-way recency, giving 416 bytes. These conventions describe the implemented logical state.

The candidate consequently adds 24 metadata bytes relative to direct mapping, or approximately 6.82% of D0's metadata. The same difference is approximately 0.59% of the common 4,096-byte data capacity. Those percentages have different denominators and answer different questions. Neither percentage measures chip-area overhead. The more defensible primary statement is the absolute difference together with the accounting assumptions. Reporting only a small percentage could obscure additional lookup logic; reporting only a large metadata percentage could exaggerate its relationship to payload capacity. Explicit denominators prevent a mathematically correct percentage from becoming a misleading architectural claim.

Reuse provides another connection to the sensor model. A sequential one-pass read touches eight aligned four-byte words per 32-byte line and then advances. In the frozen trace this produces a 12.5% measured miss rate regardless of the tested placement. A moving-average pattern repeatedly accesses recent samples and therefore has a different reuse structure. However, changing associativity or an index cannot create reuse in a stream that never returns to completed lines. This separates a placement problem from a lack-of-reuse problem. Maya's reuse-oriented treatment motivates this distinction without requiring its data-store policy to be implemented in the project.

The write policy makes the comparison more concrete. Under write-allocate, a write miss fills a line even if the trace represents a write-only acquisition stream. Under write-back, a dirty victim subsequently creates another line transfer. W1-write therefore moves 800,000 measured bytes, whereas W1-read moves 400,000 bytes despite the same miss rate. These transfers are properties of the declared model. A bypassing policy could change them, but bypassing was disabled across all designs before the official batch. Introducing it after the fact would change the experiment rather than merely improve the presentation of the selected candidate.

The reference also clarifies why an additional conventional two-way control matters. DAI-v1 and D1 share data capacity, associativity, tag width, and replacement-state accounting. Differences in their observed results can therefore be related more closely to index regrouping than differences between DAI-v1 and D0. In W5 with two blocks, both two-way designs remove the direct-mapped conflict. With four or eight blocks, D1 cannot retain the entire competing conventional set, while the XOR candidate distributes the group. This is a narrower and more informative explanation than attributing every gain over D0 to an AI-generated expression.

The study's resource conclusions remain deliberately bounded. No shared last-level hierarchy, coherence protocol, secure random remapping, or separate tag/data store is reproduced. The packed metadata estimate is sufficient to disclose a modeled storage trade-off but insufficient to demonstrate practical implementation efficiency. Time and budget constraints prevented hardware testing, leaving physical lookup delay and energy unmeasured. The publication is therefore used to frame the comparison accurately, while the report's empirical statements refer only to the four implemented organizations. This preserves the distinction between learning from a resource-accounting method and claiming equivalence to the referenced architecture.

### Study conclusion

**The Maya Cache: A Storage-efficient and Secure Fully-associative Last-level Cache** establishes the relevance of distinguishing payload capacity, metadata, and implementation resources. For Q10, it supports transparent accounting rather than a claim of reproduced secure caching. The candidate's 376-byte metadata estimate accompanies, rather than disappears behind, the equal-data-capacity comparison.

## LIT-003 - Scatter and Split Securely: Defeating Cache Contention and Occupancy Attacks

**Authors:** Lukas Giner, Stefan Steinegger, Antoon Purnal, Maria Eichlseder, Thomas Unterluggauer, Stefan Mangard, and Daniel Gruss. **Publication:** IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy, 2273-2287, 2023. **Assigned responsibility:** Trương Tô Dân, QE210127. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP46215.2023.10179440). [Author full text](https://antoonpurnal.github.io/files/pdf/SassCache.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: Section 8 includes gem5 and trace-based experiments. A reported gem5 configuration has a 1-MiB, 16-way L2 with 64-byte lines. Workloads include MiBench, lmbench, scimark2, GAP, and SPEC2017 evaluation. Sections 4-6 describe the security mechanism. Its protection objective permits hit-rate costs.

SassCache addresses contention and occupancy attacks through secure skewed associative placement and keyed index mapping. The work combines a security-oriented mapping construction with analysis of the resulting cache behavior. Its objective differs from simply maximizing hit rate: an isolation mechanism may incur a performance cost while serving its security purpose. The paper's construction, security discussion, and experimental evaluation provide the relevant context. No security result or performance penalty from that implementation is treated as a measurement of the project's candidate. The connection is conceptual: placement can be optimized under different objectives, and those objectives determine how an outcome is judged.

Skewed placement is distinct from conventional set associativity. In a conventional set-associative cache, each block selects a set and can occupy any way within that set. In a skewed organization, different ways or banks can use different index functions, creating overlapping candidate groups. This difference affects victim selection and recency accounting. A field called LRU does not automatically define a correct replacement policy across overlapping groups. The project considered skewed placement among the AI-proposed options but selected a static XOR two-way design whose eligible set and exact replacement state could be specified and verified with a simpler common engine.

That selection is a feasibility decision rather than empirical evidence that skewed caches are inferior. The official experiment contains D0, D1, D2, and DAI-v1; it contains no implemented skewed candidate. Consequently, the report can describe why the selected design was manageable within scope, but cannot rank an unimplemented skewed design using BATCH01 results. The same distinction applies to the two unselected proposals generally. Their qualitative advantages and disadvantages are part of design selection history. They are not additional experimental data points or evidence that the final choice was globally optimal among all possible mappings.

The published objective also prevents an inappropriate interpretation of conflict reduction. A layout that disperses ordinary requests may reduce misses in one trace, while a keyed randomized construction has the separate task of limiting an attacker's ability to predict conflicts. DAI-v1 is deterministic and public. Its set equation does not constitute a cryptographic construction. Blocks mapping to the same candidate set can be computed directly from it. The small validation sequence involving blocks 0, 65, and 130 demonstrates precisely such a collision group. This is compatible with functional correctness and simultaneously rules out a security claim based merely on the use of XOR.

The official W5 cases are constructed to expose conventional index conflicts. D0 maps each competing block to the same line. D1 and D2 retain the group in a conventional set and succeed only when their ways accommodate it. DAI-v1 maps the declared group into several sets and records zero measured misses after warmup. This is a useful explanation of an observed mechanism, but it is not evidence that arbitrary occupancy patterns become harmless. Because the stride was declared before selection and favors the selected mapping, the finding has a clear boundary. It does not establish broad optimization of real sensor addresses.

The interleaved sensor family has another important boundary. Four contiguous one-KiB buffers constitute a total four-KiB working region. Their selected placement permits all tested designs to retain the data after warmup. W4's round-robin trace and all ten random-selection traces therefore produce zero measured misses for every design. Interleaving requests in a single ordered trace does not simulate multicore execution, simultaneous arrivals, coherence, or security domains. The project uses interleaving to represent selection among sensor buffers. The absence of measured conflict in that arrangement supplies no evidence about multi-tenant isolation or the behavior of adversarially arranged buffers.

Evaluation terminology follows these distinctions. A candidate can be functionally correct without improving the primary metric. A security architecture can meet its intended protection objective while having a different hit-rate cost. A synthetic counterexample can demonstrate a limitation without becoming an estimate of application prevalence. In BATCH01, deterministic cases are measured once and the random cases vary only the declared selection seed. These observations permit a descriptive comparison under the frozen conditions. They do not permit an inferential claim about the proportion of real weighing stations that would benefit from hashed placement, nor a pooled claim that AI wins a particular percentage of applications.

The publication remains directly relevant because it makes the mapping objective and organization explicit. Its contribution to Q10 is a sharper distinction among set associativity, skewed candidate locations, security, and ordinary miss behavior. The selected experiment intentionally leaves cryptographic mapping and concurrent security domains outside its implementation. Hardware validation was also infeasible within the available time and budget. The resulting scope is modest but assessable: the candidate's functional state transitions and placement-specific behavior are evaluated, while unimplemented architectures and unmeasured protection properties remain outside the conclusions.

### Study conclusion

**Scatter and Split Securely: Defeating Cache Contention and Occupancy Attacks** supports the analysis of placement objectives and skewed-organization complexity. It does not supply evidence that DAI-v1 is secure or that a rejected skewed candidate performs worse. Its strongest application is the careful limitation of what the implemented mapping experiment establishes.

## LIT-004 - Tartan: Microarchitecting a Robotic Processor

**Authors:** Mohammad Bakhshalipour and Phillip B. Gibbons. **Publication:** ISCA 2024, 548-565, 2024. **Assigned responsibility:** Trần Châu Hoàng Nam, QE210145. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISCA59077.2024.00047). [Institutional full text](https://www.pdl.cmu.edu/PDL-FTP/associated/Tartan-ISCA24.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: the study evaluates robotic processing rather than a synthetic weighing application. Sections III-A, VII, and VIII-D and Figure 5 distinguish application workloads and FCP. Component-level evaluation is separated from the full processor, which contains additional acceleration and prefetching mechanisms.

Tartan examines processor support for robotic applications through several architectural mechanisms. Its intra-application cache partitioning component relates competing paths within an application to cache indexing and replacement metadata. The paper also includes mechanisms beyond cache placement, so an end-to-end performance result cannot be attributed solely to that component. The relevant source locations are the processor overview, the intra-application partitioning section, and the evaluation. The citation uses the 2024 proceedings edition; a later distribution date on a repository-generated cover is not treated as the conference year. The work is included for application-aware experimental reasoning rather than as a direct sensor-cache baseline.

The distinction between an application label and an access pattern is particularly useful. A robot, a weighing station, and a generic stream processor can each contain sequential transfers, repeated windows, competing buffers, or irregular lookups. Their names alone do not determine locality. Q10 therefore defines operations and addresses explicitly instead of treating a verbal description of continuous sensing as sufficient input. W1 represents one-pass sequential acquisition behavior; W2 represents repeated traversal of circular storage; W3 represents updating a sample and reading a recent window; W4 represents selection among four sensor buffers. W5 is separately labeled as constructed conflict stress.

The W3 definition illustrates why application semantics require a precise trace rule. A cycle first writes the current sample and then reads the newest-to-oldest window, including the newly written sample. Historical sample slots are assumed to exist in main memory before cycle zero, while the cache starts empty. No hidden initialization trace warms the cache. Each trace ends after 110,000 demand accesses, even if that truncates a sampling cycle. This rule determines the measured reuse and the relationship between write and read accesses. It is more informative than a statement that the simulator processes a moving average, because the simulator does not compute numerical averages.

Measurement units also affect the interpretation of application realism. All official cases contain 100,000 measured memory accesses, but they do not represent the same number of physical samples or elapsed seconds. A W3 cycle consumes N+1 accesses. With a window of 2,048 samples, the entire 110,000-access trace covers only 53 complete cycles and a partial cycle. The 10,000-access warmup spans only a few such cycles. Consequently, the long-window result cannot establish steady-state behavior, sampling-rate feasibility, or a particular response deadline. Equal demand counts support a controlled cache comparison while leaving these application-level questions unresolved.

Isolation of architectural contributions is another relevant principle. DAI-v1 changes both associativity and indexing relative to D0, so its entire improvement over D0 cannot be attributed to XOR alone. D1 shares its two-way organization and replacement policy while retaining conventional indexing. D2 supplies a higher-associativity control. Prefetching and bypassing remain disabled in every organization. This produces an interpretable comparison without introducing additional mechanisms that would obscure the cause of a miss-rate difference. The project does not implement Tartan's application semantics or partitioning policy; it adopts a similarly explicit distinction between an application context and the component being evaluated.

The selected sensor layout limits what can be learned from W4. Four contiguous one-KiB buffers occupy exactly the common four-KiB data capacity. Under the chosen base address, all tested designs retain the working region after warmup. Round-robin selection and all ten random-selection seeds produce zero measured misses. This outcome is not evidence that sensor interleaving never creates conflicts. It shows that the frozen arrangement does not distinguish these mappings in measured miss rate. Randomizing selection order explores one source of variation while holding layout constant. It cannot substitute for a separate study of alignment, separation between buffers, or other working-set sizes.

The experiment retains this uninformative comparison rather than modifying the layout after observing it. That decision preserves the chronology of selection, workload freeze, and official measurement. A later layout sensitivity study could add distinct preregistered inputs, but those would be a new experiment. The same discipline applies to W5: its favorable conflict structure is declared rather than described as a surprising discovery about real devices. Reporting both the equal-rate families and the favorable stress cases produces a more defensible contribution than selecting only plots in which the candidate wins. The resulting negative evidence is part of the research finding.

Tartan is consequently relevant to the design of interpretable workloads and component-level evaluation. Its robotic processor speedups do not predict the behavior of this synthetic data-cache model. The project has no executable weighing application, real sensor stream, processor timing model, or measured hardware prototype. Time and financial resources prevented hardware validation. The practical implication is conditional: memory patterns are represented explicitly enough to explain placement behavior, but the relationship between those patterns and an actual deployed station remains a question for further measurement rather than a conclusion supplied by the robotics literature.

### Study conclusion

**Tartan: Microarchitecting a Robotic Processor** supports application-aware workload definition and separation of architectural contributions. For Q10, its value lies in explaining what each synthetic family represents and what it omits. It does not establish a robotic or weighing-station speedup for the selected cache mapping.

## LIT-005 - From Optimal to Practical: Efficient Micro-op Cache Replacement Policies for Data Center Applications

**Authors:** Kan Zhu, Yilong Zhao, Yufei Gao, Peter Braun, Tanvir Ahmed Khan, Heiner Litz, Baris Kasikci, and Shuwen Deng. **Publication:** HPCA 2025, 716-731, 2025. **Assigned responsibility:** Trần Châu Hoàng Nam, QE210145. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/HPCA61900.2025.00060). [Author full text](https://www.thu-haslab.org/publication/co-public/2025-from-optimal-to-practical/19_HPCA2025_micro-op_cache.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: Section VI-A and Tables I-II describe Scarab experiments with a processor model close to AMD Zen 3 and eleven data-center applications. Figures 8-9 distinguish policy results. The comparison includes an offline reference and a practical profile-guided policy; neither is the submitted data-cache index.

This paper studies replacement in micro-operation caches. FLACK is an offline reference that considers properties specific to that structure, while FURBYS is a practical profile-guided policy intended to approximate desirable behavior. The distinction between an offline reference and a realizable mechanism is the central connection to Q10. The source's abstract, policy sections, and evaluation establish that context. Micro-operation caches differ from fixed-line data caches, including in the consequences and structure of misses. Published miss reductions and processor-level efficiency results are therefore not transferred to the present experiment, which models aligned four-byte demands to 32-byte data lines.

An offline algorithm can inspect future accesses that a runtime controller has not yet observed. This makes it useful as a reference but changes the meaning of its performance. The Q10 candidate uses the current byte address and resident cache state only. Its static index does not inspect the remaining trace, obtain future reuse distance, or choose a policy retrospectively. AI was involved before evaluation in proposing and critiquing designs, not during each access. Consequently, describing DAI-v1 as an online learned predictor or a near-optimal replacement policy would misidentify the implemented mechanism and overstate its relationship to this publication.

The project's reference model serves a different purpose from an offline optimal policy. It stores full block identifiers in per-set recency lists and implements the same declared replacement behavior. The main simulator stores compressed tags and recency bits or ranks. Agreement checks whether two representations produce the same intended cache transitions; it does not estimate an unattainable optimal miss rate. Submission QA compares measured counters for all 96 official inputs with this reference representation. Such a check is valuable because implementation bugs can produce persuasive numerical improvements. It remains an AI-assisted correctness comparison rather than an independent proof that the modeled policy is optimal.

The difference is especially important for dirty victims. A reference model retaining full block identifiers can directly recover a victim's byte base. The compressed XOR model must reconstruct it from tag and set. A test that compares only the final miss total could overlook a wrong writeback address. Small validation traces therefore compare victim identity and writeback base as well as hit/miss behavior. The official counters additionally preserve read misses, write misses, fills, evictions, and dirty writebacks. This combination supports functional interpretation of the results without treating a single aggregate metric as sufficient evidence of a correct implementation.

Profile information would also require an explicit evaluation protocol. A design trained on the same measured trace could obtain an advantage unavailable under ordinary deployment assumptions. BATCH01 has no training phase, profile table, or learned runtime parameters. The candidate and all workload details were frozen before official results. However, the broad stress pattern was known during candidate selection. The design process was therefore not blind to the workload specification. This qualified chronology permits a claim of prior design specification while preventing a stronger claim that the candidate's favorable W5 behavior was discovered on wholly unseen access structure.

The measured W5 outcome illustrates why reference context matters. The XOR candidate records zero misses after warmup, while D0 records 100% for each declared competing group. This does not mean the candidate has achieved an optimal architecture for every workload. Conventional D1 also achieves zero measured misses for two blocks, and D2 does so for two and four. All designs match one another in W1-W4. Furthermore, the validation counterexample demonstrates an address group that disadvantages the XOR index. These observations collectively support a narrow placement explanation, rather than an inference from a favorable stress result to universal superiority or near-optimal cache management.

Metric comparison requires equally explicit denominators. A change from 100% to 0% miss rate is a reduction of 100 percentage points and a 100% relative miss reduction. It is not a 100% execution-time reduction. A baseline with zero measured misses has no defined relative miss-reduction percentage. The project's analysis stores that quantity as null and preserves the absolute rates. This distinction remains necessary even when an offline reference or profile-guided policy is being discussed, because percentages that refer to different events or baselines cannot be compared by appearance alone. Original counters preserve the information needed to recompute the reported values.

The paper is retained for its clear separation between reference opportunity and implementable behavior. Q10 applies that separation to its own terminology: the implemented candidate is static; the reference oracle checks declared semantics; the AMAT table is a sensitivity calculation; and execution logs are not hardware latency measurements. No frontend pipeline, micro-operation decoding behavior, or profile-guided replacement mechanism is reproduced. This disciplined separation makes the related study useful without expanding the simulator beyond its validated scope or borrowing a performance conclusion that depends on a different cache structure and experimental environment.

### Study conclusion

**From Optimal to Practical: Efficient Micro-op Cache Replacement Policies for Data Center Applications** supports the distinction between offline reference knowledge, profile-based mechanisms, and implementable online decisions. Its role in Q10 is methodological. It does not provide an optimality guarantee or transferable micro-operation-cache performance figure for DAI-v1.

## LIT-006 - EMISSARY: Enhanced Miss Awareness Replacement Policy for L2 Instruction Caching

**Authors:** Nayana Prasad Nagendra, Bhargav Reddy Godala, Ishita Chaturvedi, Atmn Patel, Svilen Kanev, Tipp Moseley, Jared Stark, Gilles A. Pokam, Simone Campanoni, and David I. August. **Publication:** ISCA 2023, Article 62, 1-13, 2023. **Assigned responsibility:** Trần Châu Hoàng Nam, QE210145. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3579371.3589097). [Author full text](https://liberty.princeton.edu/Publications/isca23_emissary.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: Sections 5-6 describe full-system gem5 evaluation with server workloads and an instruction-cache baseline. These processor-level experiments measure consequences beyond instruction-line miss frequency. Article number 62 and the DOI identify the publication despite differences in pagination across distributed versions.

EMISSARY examines instruction-cache replacement with awareness of the consequences of misses rather than treating every miss as equally costly. Its instruction-fetch setting and processor evaluation distinguish it from the project's single-level data-cache model. The relevant source material is the policy description and the evaluation of the instruction-caching mechanism. The project does not reproduce that policy or use its published processor gains as evidence about sensor streams. The study is included because it supports a precise methodological distinction: a count of cache misses, by itself, does not determine execution time, memory traffic, or energy expenditure.

That distinction is visible directly in BATCH01. Sequential reading and writing each have a 12.5% measured miss rate in all four designs. Nevertheless, their measured traffic differs. The read case transfers 400,000 bytes, while the write case transfers 800,000 bytes. A miss causes a full-line fill in both cases. Replacing a valid dirty line adds a writeback in the write stream. Equal miss frequency therefore does not imply equal transfer volume. This provides a concrete reason for reporting separate counters rather than interpreting one rate as a complete account of memory-system behavior or the cost of processing a sample.

The traffic definition is intentionally limited. It counts measured fills and dirty evictions in units of 32 bytes. Cache state, including dirty flags, survives warmup, so a measured writeback may concern a line first dirtied before the measurement boundary. Warmup transfers themselves are excluded. There is no final flush of dirty lines still resident at termination. These conventions make the traffic number reproducible, but the number is not total lifetime traffic for a complete application. Changing the boundary or adding a final flush could alter a write-heavy result. The report therefore states the convention alongside the metric instead of allowing an implicit lifecycle assumption.

The project's AMAT calculation is similarly explicit. Estimated access time equals an assumed lookup cost plus miss rate times an additional miss penalty. The penalty values are 20, 50, and 100 cycles. The common-lookup case assigns one cycle to every design; two additional cases raise the candidate's assumed lookup cost to 1.5 or 2 cycles while retaining one cycle for the controls. These values are analysis assumptions frozen before official measurement, not results of synthesis or processor timing. The simple formula also omits writeback stalls and overlap, so it cannot fully represent the difference between the read and write streams.

For W1-W4, the candidate has the same miss rate as the controls. Its estimated AMAT is therefore equal under equal lookup assumptions and higher when its assumed lookup cost is higher. W5 gives a different conditional outcome because the miss-rate gap is large. With a 50-cycle penalty, direct mapping has an estimate of 51 cycles per access, whereas a two-cycle candidate lookup yields an estimate of 2. This comparison illustrates the sensitivity formula. It does not establish a measured 25.5-fold application speedup, because no processor execution, stall overlap, instruction stream, or hardware critical path has been modeled.

The publication also highlights the importance of preserving the level of explanation. EMISSARY concerns instruction-fetch costs; Q10 concerns demand data lines. A statement that different misses may have different consequences is a transferable methodological principle. A numerical performance result from an instruction cache is not transferable evidence about a weighing application. Similarly, a Python simulator that completes quickly does not imply a fast hardware controller. The run logs record execution duration for provenance only. They are excluded from the simulated hardware latency claims and are not used to select the winning cache organization.

Small improvements require the same care as large ones. At N=512, the frozen W3 trace produces only 24 measured misses per 100,000 accesses for every design, or 0.024%. The original counts distinguish this result from a rounded zero and preserve the denominator. At N=2048, 12,537 measured misses correspond to 12.537%, still identically across the organizations. These figures characterize the selected finite prefixes rather than an asymptotic or steady-state processing regime. Reporting counts with rates prevents small values, percentage changes, and warmup exclusions from being interpreted as stronger evidence than the trace actually supplies.

The study's practical scope remains constrained by available resources. Time and financial limitations prevented implementation and validation on physical cache hardware. Actual lookup latency, power, area, and application execution time remain unknown. EMISSARY therefore strengthens the report's restraint: miss rate is the primary simulated behavior; traffic is a separate measured model quantity; AMAT is an assumed-latency sensitivity calculation; and hardware benefit remains unverified. This separation gives the experiment a useful result even when the selected candidate fails to improve the main sensor-oriented cases, because the result can be assessed without confusing event frequency with overall performance.

### Study conclusion

**EMISSARY: Enhanced Miss Awareness Replacement Policy for L2 Instruction Caching** supports distinguishing miss frequency from miss consequences. In Q10, it motivates separate traffic reporting and qualified AMAT interpretation. It does not justify hardware speedup or energy claims from the simulator's hit/miss counters.

## LIT-007 - CHROME: Concurrency-Aware Holistic Cache Management Framework with Online Reinforcement Learning

**Authors:** Xiaoyang Lu, Hamed Najafi, Jason Liu, and Xian-He Sun. **Publication:** HPCA 2024, 1154-1167, 2024. **Assigned responsibility:** Dương Quốc Thịnh, QE210121. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/HPCA57654.2024.00090). [Author full text](https://xiaoyang-lu.github.io/files/HPCA2024/CHROME_paper.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: ChampSim configurations cover four, eight, and sixteen cores, with SPEC2006/2017 and GAP workloads. Sections IV-VII describe the framework and evaluation; Table III reports management overhead. Its multi-mechanism, multicore comparison differs from the single-level static-index experiment.

CHROME presents holistic cache management using online reinforcement learning and concurrency-aware feedback. Its management framework coordinates replacement and bypassing with prefetching rather than studying a static set-index expression alone. The relevant source locations are the framework description, learning formulation, and evaluation. This makes the publication directly relevant to the broad subject of AI-supported cache management, but it also establishes an important difference from Q10. The present project uses conversational AI during design and implementation. Its deployed simulator contains no learning agent, reward update, neural predictor, or runtime training phase. Published reinforcement-learning gains are not evidence that the selected static mapping will improve sensor processing.

The term AI-assisted therefore identifies a research process rather than a hardware mechanism. Three alternatives were proposed, followed by critique and final selection. The selected specification was frozen before implementation and official evaluation. During simulation, each demand address is processed by deterministic mapping and exact replacement rules. Its behavior does not adapt to the measured miss rate or change after a reward signal. This is an important boundary because a paper title about AI-assisted cache mapping could otherwise be mistaken for an online learning architecture. The report and source describe the same static mechanism, and the preserved prompts document the earlier role of AI.

Online learning would require additional information and state. A policy needs an observation representation, an action space, an update rule, a feedback definition, and a treatment of initialization. Those requirements carry storage and execution costs that depend on the chosen organization. BATCH01 deliberately contains none of these runtime elements. The candidate uses its current address and ordinary resident cache state. Consequently, the project does not measure training convergence, prediction accuracy, exploration costs, or adaptation to changing phases. These omissions are consistent with the selected scope rather than deficiencies hidden behind an AI label. They also prevent borrowing reinforcement-learning terminology to make a simple design appear more sophisticated.

The distinction has implications for fair controls. A holistic scheme may simultaneously alter replacement, bypassing, and prefetching. Its improvement over a conventional baseline reflects the resulting combination unless components are isolated. Q10 holds write-back, write-allocate, exact LRU, disabled prefetching, and disabled bypassing constant. D1 and the candidate additionally share two-way associativity. Their comparison therefore addresses set regrouping more directly than the candidate-to-D0 comparison. The latter changes both associativity and indexing. This explicit control structure supports a causal explanation within the model while avoiding a stronger assertion that every difference is the isolated effect of a particular XOR operation.

The resource scale reinforces this caution. The modeled payload capacity is only 4 KiB. An auxiliary table of several kilobytes would be substantial relative to that capacity, even if it appeared small in a large last-level cache. The candidate instead adds 24 metadata bytes relative to D0 under the declared packed accounting and matches D1's estimate. That estimate does not include a learned controller because no such controller exists. It also does not measure XOR circuitry or two-way selection delay. The report preserves the distinction between counted logical state, unmeasured physical implementation resources, and the additional metadata that a different learning-based design might require.

The measured pattern provides a restrained outcome for the AI-assisted process. The candidate matches all conventional miss rates in W1-W4 and improves over direct mapping only in W5. That does not demonstrate that AI design assistance is ineffective in general, because the project contains one selected design, one frozen workload suite, and no controlled comparison of human-only and AI-assisted design processes. Conversely, the W5 improvement does not establish the general quality of AI-generated architecture. It demonstrates that this specified mapping behaves favorably for a declared conflict pattern. The process contribution is an auditable sequence from proposal to implementation to measured evaluation.

AI involvement also qualifies the validation evidence. The same broader assistance process contributed to source code, tests, analysis, and report preparation. A separate full-block reference representation reduces the chance that compressed-tag mistakes go unnoticed, and all official counter comparisons passed during submission QA. Nevertheless, these checks are not independent human review. Approval and execution authorization are attributed to Trương Tô Dân, while direct tool execution is attributed to Codex/Python. Existing prompts, responses, and session records are preserved, but a complete chat export and a verified exact model identifier are absent. The documentation states these limitations instead of filling the gaps with inferred metadata.

CHROME is thus retained as an example of runtime machine learning whose costs and scope clarify what Q10 does and does not implement. Its architectural conclusions are not reproduced. Hardware implementation and validation were infeasible within the project's time and budget, so the present evidence remains a trace-driven comparison with assumed-latency sensitivity. The reference helps separate the meaning of AI assistance, the mechanism inside a controller, and the strength of experimental evidence. Those distinctions make the project's modest result interpretable without implying online intelligence, measured adaptation, or a demonstrated application-level speedup.

### Study conclusion

**CHROME: Concurrency-Aware Holistic Cache Management Framework with Online Reinforcement Learning** supports discussion of learning-related state, coordinated mechanisms, and evaluation controls. Its main role is to distinguish online AI cache management from offline AI-assisted design. DAI-v1 implements the latter process and a static runtime mapping only.

## LIT-008 - A Prefetch-Adaptive Intelligent Cache Replacement Policy Based on Machine Learning

**Authors:** Hui-Jing Yang, Juan Fang, Min Cai, and Zhi Cai. **Publication:** Journal of Computer Science and Technology, 38(2), 391-404, 2023. **Assigned responsibility:** Dương Quốc Thịnh, QE210121. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11390-022-1573-3). [Publisher full text](https://jcst.ict.ac.cn/en/article/pdf/preview/10.1007/s11390-022-1573-3.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: ChampSim/CRC2 experiments use 27 SPEC2006/2017 programs. Sections 3-4, Table 1, and Figures 7-8 describe predictors, resource budget, and baselines. Section 4.2.1 distinguishes LRU without prefetching from LRU with SPP, so their reported improvement percentages are not interchangeable.

The paper introduces Prefetch-Adaptive Intelligent Cache Replacement Policy, or PAIC. It distinguishes prefetch and demand requests through separate prediction mechanisms and evaluates their interaction with cache replacement. Its central relevance is that a policy's outcome depends on the surrounding memory-system mechanisms and comparison baseline. The source's predictor description, experimental setup, and evaluation provide the appropriate context. The publication belongs to the March 2023 journal edition despite the string 022 in its DOI and its earlier acceptance date. It therefore meets the project's publication interval. The DOI identifier itself is not used as evidence of publication year.

Prefetching changes the sequence of events observed by a cache. An incoming line may have been requested speculatively rather than by a demand access, and the usefulness of retaining it can differ. A replacement policy aware of that distinction is not equivalent to changing an index in a demand-only simulator. Q10 disables prefetching in every design. Its input trace contains only declared reads and writes, and every miss fills one line under write-allocate. Consequently, PAIC's reported improvement over a particular processor baseline cannot be interpreted as a predicted improvement for DAI-v1. The mechanisms, requests, and measured execution contexts differ.

This distinction also applies to published percentages. A gain over an LRU configuration without a particular supporting mechanism does not isolate the contribution of replacement under otherwise identical conditions. The project therefore retains explicit configuration snapshots for each run. Cache capacity, line size, write policy, replacement, prefetch, and bypass settings remain visible alongside the mapping choice. DAI-v1 and D1 share the same ways and replacement rule, while D0 provides the requested direct-mapped baseline. This comparison structure makes the interpretation of the results more precise than a single favorable percentage attached to an unspecified baseline.

The distinction between read misses and write misses is another useful connection. A predictor-aware cache may treat request types differently, while Q10's simpler engine preserves a common allocation rule. A write sets a resident line's dirty flag; a read does not clear it. On a miss, an invalid location is preferred. Replacing a valid dirty line generates a full-line writeback, with the victim address reconstructed before the tag changes. These rules determine memory traffic independently of whether a proposal is described as intelligent. They also supply observable state transitions for validation. Correct request accounting is a prerequisite for interpreting any claimed management improvement.

Initialization requires equally careful treatment. Every official run begins with an empty cache, performs 10,000 warmup accesses, then resets counters while retaining valid entries, dirty flags, and recency. No design inherits a warmed state from a previous configuration or seed. The W3 historical samples are assumed to exist in main memory but are not preloaded into cache through hidden accesses. This distinction separates logical application history from cache initialization. A future learned policy would also need a declared rule for predictor state at the same boundary. BATCH01 has no predictor, so it makes no claim about the convergence or fairness of such additional state.

The absence of prefetching limits external validity. A real weighing program could execute on a processor whose prefetcher recognizes sequential transfers or whose hierarchy overlaps outstanding misses. The present measured miss rates would not automatically predict that system's traffic or execution time. For example, every tested design records 12.5% misses for the sequential traces, but that equality describes demand events in this model. It does not prove equal processor stalls under a different hierarchy. The report treats this restriction as a scope limitation and separates AMAT assumptions from observed counters rather than adding unsupported claims about a physical implementation.

The candidate's unfavorable assumed-latency cases further illustrate baseline discipline. In W1-W4, equal miss rates produce equal estimated AMAT only when lookup times are assumed equal. Raising the candidate's assumed lookup from one cycle to 1.5 or 2 cycles worsens that estimate under the same miss penalties. In W5, its large miss-rate difference produces a favorable conditional estimate. These calculations preserve the baseline and formula for every row of the sensitivity table. They do not combine published PAIC speedups with project miss rates, and they do not describe an unmeasured hardware lookup delay as a factual implementation cost.

PAIC is retained as a machine-learning cache-management reference with a clear request-type and baseline lesson. It supports interpretation of mechanisms and evaluation conditions, not implementation of a predictor in the submitted simulator. The report's distinction between static indexing and learned replacement remains consistent with the code, configurations, and AI records. No hardware validation was possible because time and financial resources were insufficient. Accordingly, the source strengthens the account of what is controlled and what remains unverified, while the empirical conclusions continue to originate solely from the frozen demand traces and original BATCH01 records.

### Study conclusion

**A Prefetch-Adaptive Intelligent Cache Replacement Policy Based on Machine Learning** supports careful interpretation of request types, supporting mechanisms, and baselines. Its 2023 publication is eligible, but its reported performance is not transferable to a demand-only mapping experiment. Q10 uses it to justify explicit controls and qualified conclusions.

## LIT-009 - Sketching the Path to Efficiency: Lightweight Learned Cache Replacement

**Authors:** Rana Shahout and Roy Friedman. **Publication:** OPODIS 2023 proceedings, LIPIcs 286, Article 34, 34:1-34:21; published 18 January 2024. **Assigned responsibility:** Dương Quốc Thịnh, QE210121. [Publication DOI and publisher record](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.OPODIS.2023.34). [Publisher full text](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol286-opodis2023/LIPIcs.OPODIS.2023.34/LIPIcs.OPODIS.2023.34.pdf).

### Contribution and critical appraisal

Evaluation context: Sections 3-4 describe C++ implementation with the Caffeine simulator. Table 3 compares software caches of 1K, 10K, and 100K items with frames ten times the cache size and policy combinations including LRU. Item count is not interpreted as processor-cache byte capacity.

This study presents ALPS, a learned cache-management meta-policy that uses compact sketches of access behavior over frames. Machine learning selects among conventional policies using the frame-level features, reducing reliance on storing extensive item-specific history. The paper also introduces ISketch for inter-arrival information. Its main setting is software item caching rather than fixed-line processor data caching. The relevant source material is the framework description, frame-based feature construction, and evaluation. It is included for its direct connection to machine learning, access streams, and management overhead. The distinction between software items and processor lines remains explicit in both the review and report.

Frame-level analysis provides a useful conceptual contrast to the project's static controller. A sequence can contain phases whose working sets and reuse differ. An adaptive policy may respond to those phases, but DAI-v1 uses one mapping throughout each run. Its set equation does not depend on a frame classifier or accumulated workload statistics. Therefore, the project cannot claim that the candidate detects changing phases or selects the best policy over time. The related publication instead identifies a possible dimension of future evaluation while clarifying the simplicity of the actual design. That separation avoids describing an offline plot or summary as runtime intelligence.

The unit of capacity is a major limitation on transfer. A software cache can count items whose sizes or retrieval costs differ, while Q10 contains 128 fixed 32-byte data lines. The submitted demand operations are aligned four-byte accesses and cannot cross a line boundary under the accepted input format. Invalid sizes and unaligned addresses are rejected rather than silently decomposed. Hit ratios from an item-cache experiment therefore cannot be converted directly into miss rates for the processor model by substituting an item count for a byte capacity. The report preserves the original metric context instead of mixing numerically similar percentages from incompatible caching systems.

The cost of tracking access history also depends on scale. A compact statistical structure may be economical for one cache size and large relative to a four-KiB payload. BATCH01 reports packed metadata separately from data capacity, including tag, valid, dirty, and exact recency state. It contains no frame sketches or learned-policy tables. The candidate's 376-byte estimate therefore describes only its implemented state. It is not evidence that an adaptive extension would have the same budget, lookup behavior, or verification burden. Any such extension would require a new design version and an explicit accounting of the information retained between accesses.

Frame length would create another experimental parameter. A short interval can expose variation but make estimates noisy; a long interval can combine distinct behaviors. Q10 uses one declared warmup and measurement boundary rather than selecting windows after viewing favorable results. The long W3 case illustrates why the time horizon matters: its trace contains only 53 complete sample-processing cycles and part of another, despite having 110,000 memory accesses. An analysis that ignores the distinction between memory accesses, application cycles, and elapsed time could suggest a stronger stability result than the experiment supports. No steady-state or phase-adaptation claim is made from that finite prefix.

Statistical independence is similarly constrained. The official matrix contains 24 trace groups, each evaluated under four designs. The resulting 96 records are not 96 independent samples of an application population. Only the W4-random cases vary the declared random-selection seed, with ten seeds retained for each design. Their mean and sample standard deviation of measured miss rate are both zero. This describes variation in selection order under the same contiguous-buffer layout. It says nothing about uncertainty across different sensor arrangements, capacities, address alignments, or physical installations. The group structure is retained in the raw and summary tables rather than hidden by a pooled success statistic.

Offline selection of a winning policy after observing the entire trace would also differ from an implementable frame predictor. The submitted candidate was fixed before official results and never switches during execution. The analysis scripts read completed records to summarize behavior, but those summaries do not feed back into the controller. This boundary is preserved by retaining frozen input and design hashes, original run records, and a separate analysis plan. The full submission audit verifies those identities and recomputes numerical summaries. It does not transform post-experiment interpretation into an online selection mechanism or claim that a policy chosen retrospectively was available at access time.

The reference remains useful because it connects access-stream characterization with the cost of making adaptive decisions. Its scope also reinforces the modest interpretation of Q10: the candidate is a static placement rule with demonstrated benefits in selected conflicts, equality in the tested sensor families, and unmeasured hardware implementation costs. Time and financial constraints prevented validation on physical hardware. A future frame-sensitive extension could be a distinct study, but the current evidence neither requires nor validates it. The publication thus supports a careful discussion of possible extensions while leaving the submitted experiment's methods, results, and limitations intact.

### Study conclusion

**Sketching the Path to Efficiency: Lightweight Learned Cache Replacement** supports discussion of frame statistics, adaptation, and management overhead. It is a methodological reference from software caching, not a direct processor-cache performance baseline. Its 2024 publication date is distinguished from the OPODIS 2023 conference label.

## Overall conclusion

The nine studies support three complementary themes: placement and resource accounting, application-aware evaluation and metric interpretation, and learning-related information and overhead. They do not supply an interchangeable collection of speedup figures. Security caches, robotic processors, instruction and micro-operation caches, learned replacement, and software item caches have different objectives and evaluation contexts.

The resulting Q10 contribution is a controlled, reproducible evaluation of one AI-selected static mapping. The candidate is functionally specified and checked, but its measured benefit is confined to the constructed W5 stress family. W1-W4 provide no measured miss-rate advantage. Additional metadata, assumed lookup sensitivity, synthetic inputs, fixed layouts, short sampling horizons, and absent hardware validation qualify the outcome. Insufficient time and financial resources prevented implementing and testing a hardware prototype. No general architecture optimality, application speedup, security guarantee, or measured energy saving follows from the collected data.
